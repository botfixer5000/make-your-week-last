Grok Bot weekly usage running out? 8 fixes that work

The short answer: on The X Subscription, your bots, the Grok apps and Cursor all drain one shared weekly pool, and frequent routines are the most common leak. Cap your paid extra usage first, then slow down or quiet your routines, start fresh chats, and stop bots from chatting with each other.

Why your Grok Bot usage runs out mid-week

  • It's one pool, not three. Each paid seat gets one weekly pool shared across eligible Grok and Cursor surfaces, "rather than split into separate product quotas" (X Subscription docs). Grok Bot has no separate meter (Cursor help). A heavy Cursor coding day and a busy bot team drain the same tank.
  • Different work costs different amounts. "Usage rates vary by product" (docs). Agent work is measured by steps and tokens, not by the number of messages you send (Cursor help).
  • Routines run while you sleep. Each run spends usage. An hourly schedule, a short interval, or a Slack trigger on a busy channel "can use a week of usage in a day" (Cursor help: Routines).

Nobody outside xAI publishes the pool size as a number, so be skeptical of any guide that gives you one.

Check where you stand (2 minutes)

  1. Open Usage at grok.com/dashboard. Note the percent used and the next reset date.
  2. Write the percent down at the same time tomorrow. Percent gained per day tells you how many days your week lasts. 25% a day means about four days.
  3. Note what you did that day ("2h Cursor", "big research job"). After 2 or 3 days, the heavy days stand out.

The meter shows your whole pool, not each bot. If a bot tells you "Bot X used 40%" and that number didn't come from a real screen, it's a guess.

Stop surprise charges first

When the weekly pool hits 100%, further usage becomes paid Additional Usage from prepaid credit (docs). The controls are under Additional usage credits on Billing & invoices at grok.com/dashboard/billing (docs):

  • Monthly additional usage limit: if none is set, there's no cap. Click Edit and set one. It counts the extra usage you use, not the credit you add, so buying credit doesn't raise it (docs).
  • Auto Top-up: off unless you turned it on. When on, it charges your saved card whenever credit drops below your threshold. Leave it off unless a bot does something that must never stop.
  • Prepaid credit: the balance top-ups add to. Each top-up expires 365 days after purchase if unused (Cursor help).

Legacy Cursor plans call these On-demand usage and On-demand monthly limit, and Cursor notes a legacy limit "is not a hard stop in the middle of a run" (Cursor help). Either way, set your cap a bit below what you can afford.

The 8 fixes

1. Slow down routines that don't need to be fast

Inbox or news watching: 2 or 3 times a day. Prices: once a day. Reports: weekly. Ask the bot: "Edit your routine: [name]. Run at 8:00 and 16:00."

2. Make routines quiet when nothing changed

Edit your routine "[ROUTINE NAME]":
- First check: has [THE THING YOU WATCH] changed since the last run?
- If nothing changed: end the run silently. No message to me.
- If something changed: send me at most 5 lines, with details in a file.

3. Narrow Slack triggers

Watch one channel for a mention or a specific phrase, not "any message." Only new messages trigger runs (docs).

4. Pause what you don't use

"A routine that never runs doesn't spend usage" (docs). Pause it rather than deleting it, so you can bring it back. Look for duplicates and routines you don't remember creating.

5. Don't use Test casually

Test "does real work" and spends usage. Test with a small, safe input.

6. Start a new chat for each new task

Very long conversations tend to cost more per reply, because the bot has more context to work through. That's a rule of thumb, not an official rate. Open a fresh chat with the same bot and give it two lines of context.

7. Stop bot-to-bot chatter

In group chats, @-mention the one bot you need. Tell your bots: "Only message another bot when it needs to act. No acknowledgments or progress pings."

8. Sample before you scale

Before "research 200 companies," run 3 to 5 and check the meter before and after. Then decide. Cursor's own help recommends starting small and checking usage before large or long jobs (plans and billing).

Also worth knowing: on an individual Premium, Super or Ultra plan billed in the US and bought on the web, each friend you refer who becomes a paid subscriber earns you a usage reset, added 7 days after their first payment. It's still rolling out to accounts (docs).

Fix or upgrade?

Fix the leaks first, then measure for a few days. If your week still runs out and your plan price plus last month's top-ups is more than the next tier, the next tier may be cheaper. Check current prices at docs.x.ai pricing before you switch, since tiers and prices change.

FAQ

When does Grok Bot usage reset?

Weekly. The next reset date is shown under Usage at grok.com/dashboard. Unused included usage doesn't roll over (Cursor help).

What happens when I hit the limit?

On The X Subscription you can keep going on prepaid credit, wait for the weekly reset, or upgrade (Cursor help). With no credit and no top-ups, paid usage stops until the reset.

Does Cursor use the same pool as my bots?

Yes, for eligible first-party usage on The X Subscription. Third-party models in Cursor are paid Additional Usage and aren't included in the pool (docs).

Can a bot tell me exactly how much each routine uses?

Not from the official screens. The meter shows your whole pool. You can estimate from runs per week and how heavy each run is, but treat it as an estimate.
